If you break down on the interstate

  • Get as far right as you can — exit or shoulder. Hazards on.
  • If traffic is close, stay buckled in the car. If you must get out, exit away from traffic and stand behind a barrier.
  • Note the last exit, mile marker, and direction before you call.
  • Free highway courtesy patrols (like Florida's Road Rangers) can do jump starts, tire changes and a little fuel — towing beyond that is usually at your cost.
  • Never accept a tow from a truck that just "shows up" without being called — confirm the company name first.
  • Brake or steering failure? Don't drive it. Ask for a mobile assessment or a properly rated tow.

Before you accept a tow

  • Confirm the truck can handle your vehicle's weight, wheelbase and height.
  • EV? Ask for a flatbed — most electric cars shouldn't be towed with their drive wheels on the ground. Check your owner's manual.
  • AWD/4WD or low cars usually need a flatbed.
  • Get the total price up front — hook-up, per-mile and after-hours fees.
  • Ask where the car is going and get the driver's company name and number.
